You’ve already done something kind by getting the kitten out and taking it to two vets. **You do not have to keep it**, especially if you’re already exhausted. A kitten only a few days old usually needs: - Warmth at all times - Kitten milk replacer every 2–3 hours, including overnight - Help urinating and defecating after feeds - Close monitoring and rapid veterinary care if it declines That’s essentially round-the-clock neonatal care. The most responsible next step may be to contact local rescues, shelters, neonatal kitten fosters, or veterinary clinics and explicitly ask for an **experienced bottle-baby foster**. Post in local rescue groups too—sometimes individual foster carers respond faster than organizations. Until you find help: - **Keep it warm** with a wrapped heating pad on low under half the box, so it can move away. - **Never feed a cold kitten.** - Use only kitten milk replacer—no cow’s milk. - Don’t force milk by syringe, as it can enter the lungs. Ask a vet to demonstrate feeding if they haven’t already. If no experienced carer can take it, ask one of the vets honestly whether treatment is realistic or whether the kitten is suffering. With such a poor prognosis, **humane euthanasia can sometimes be the kindest option**; that would not mean you failed. > You don’t need to sacrifice your health to prove you care. Finding appropriate help—or making a compassionate end-of-life decision with a vet—is still caring for it.
