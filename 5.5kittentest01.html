A newborn kitten needs care **every 2–3 hours, day and night**, plus warmth, feeding, and help peeing/pooping. If you’re inexperienced and depleted, the kitten’s best chance is likely with a **neonatal kitten foster/rescue**. ## What I’d do now **Try to transfer it ASAP** to someone experienced: - Call local animal rescues and shelters and say: “I found a days-old orphaned kitten. It needs a neonatal/bottle-baby foster urgently.” - Ask the vets you visited if they know any **bottle-baby foster networks**. - Search/post in local Facebook groups: “neonatal kitten foster,” “bottle baby kitten rescue,” “TNR/rescue [your city].” - Contact animal control or humane society if rescues are closed. > Keeping it temporarily while you find help is generous. Keeping it long-term is only realistic if you can commit to round-the-clock neonatal care. ## If you have to care for it tonight 1. **Keep it warm first.** Use a heating pad on low under half the box, or warm water bottle wrapped in a towel. The kitten must be able to crawl away from heat. 2. **Do not feed it if it’s cold.** Cold kittens can’t digest and can aspirate. 3. **Use kitten formula only.** KMR or another kitten milk replacer. **No cow’s milk.** 4. **Feed belly-down, never on its back.** Go slow. If milk comes from the nose, stop immediately. 5. **Stimulate pee/poop after feeding.** Gently rub the genital area with a warm damp cotton pad/tissue. 6. **Emergency signs:** Limpness, cold body, gasping, crying nonstop, milk from nose, seizures, blue/gray gums, or refusal to eat = emergency vet/rescue now. ## Should you keep it? Honestly: **only if you can get help or you’re ready for an exhausting, high-risk commitment.** If not, the most loving choice is to hand it to an experienced foster/rescue as soon as possible.
