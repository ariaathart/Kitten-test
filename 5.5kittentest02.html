That’s an intense, exhausting thing to walk into — and it’s okay to admit you may not be able to do this. **Should you keep it?** Only if you can realistically handle **round-the-clock neonatal care** for weeks: feeding every 2–3 hours, keeping it warm, helping it pee/poop, monitoring for crashes, and possibly losing it despite doing everything right. If you’re already exhausted, the most responsible choice may be to **transfer it to someone experienced** — a neonatal kitten foster, rescue, vet tech, shelter foster network, or cat/TNR group. > Surrendering or handing it to an experienced foster is not “giving up.” It may be the kitten’s best chance — and it protects you from burning out or feeling crushed if it doesn’t survive. ## What to do right now Call/message urgently and say: **“I found a days-old orphaned kitten. It is unweaned and needs a neonatal bottle foster immediately.”** Try: - Local kitten rescues - Humane society / SPCA - TNR groups - Facebook local cat rescue groups - Emergency vet clinics - Vet offices — ask if any techs foster neonates - Animal control, but ask specifically whether they have **neonatal fosters** If you can’t find placement tonight, keep it alive short-term: ## Temporary care tonight - **Keep it warm first.** A cold kitten cannot digest food safely. Use a heating pad on low under half the box, or warm water bottle wrapped in towel. It must be able to crawl away from heat. - **Do not give cow’s milk.** Use kitten milk replacer — KMR or similar. - Feed **belly-down**, never on its back. - Feed tiny amounts every **2–3 hours**, including overnight. - After feeding, gently rub the genital area with warm damp cotton/tissue to help it **pee/poop**. - Keep it in a quiet box with soft towels, away from other pets. - If it’s limp, cold, gasping, crying nonstop, refusing to suckle, or has diarrhea/blood — that’s emergency vet territory. ## Emotionally: you’re allowed to choose limits A newborn with poor survival odds may die even with perfect care. That doesn’t mean you failed. If your gut says, “I can’t do this,” then your next loving act is finding a rescue/foster or asking a vet about the most humane options if the kitten is suffering. For now: **warmth + urgent rescue calls**. Don’t try to carry this alone if you’re already depleted.
