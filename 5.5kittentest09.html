I’m really sorry — that’s a lot to suddenly be carrying. **If you’re already exhausted and you have no neonatal kitten experience, the kindest choice may be to transfer it to an experienced neonatal foster/rescue ASAP**, not necessarily “keep it” yourself. Newborn kittens need round-the-clock care, and a 15% survival estimate means this may be emotionally and physically brutal even if you do everything right. You are **not cruel** if you can’t take this on. Getting it to someone equipped is still saving it. ## What to do now **Do not put it back outside or in the dumpster.** For now, keep it safe and warm while you look for help. Call/message urgently: - Local cat rescues - Shelters and humane societies — ask specifically for a **bottle-baby/neonatal foster** - Vet clinics — ask if they know any neonatal fosters - Local Facebook groups: “cat rescue,” “TNR,” “community cats,” “kitten rescue” - Emergency vet hospitals Say: > “I found a newborn kitten, likely only a few days old, alone in a dumpster. Two vets gave poor survival odds. I need an experienced neonatal foster immediately.” ## If you have to care for it short-term The priorities are: 1. **Warmth first** A cold kitten cannot digest food. Put it in a box/carrier with blankets and a heating pad on low under **half** the box, so it can crawl away if too warm. Aim for warm, not hot. 2. **Kitten formula only** Use **KMR/kitten milk replacer**, not cow’s milk. Feed belly-down, never on its back. 3. **Feed very often** Newborns usually need feeding every **2–3 hours, including overnight**. 4. **Help it pee/poop** After feeding, gently rub the genital area with a warm damp cotton pad/tissue until it pees/poops. 5. **Get help fast if there are danger signs** Limpness, constant crying, milk from the nose, trouble breathing, refusing food, feeling cold, or bloated belly = emergency. ## My honest answer If you’re asking “should I keep it?” as in take full responsibility yourself: **only if you can realistically do 24/7 care and handle the possibility it may die despite your efforts.** If not, the best answer is: **keep it safe temporarily, but urgently hand it to a neonatal foster/rescue.** That is still a compassionate, responsible choice. And if vets believe the kitten is suffering with very little chance, it’s also okay to ask them directly about whether continued care is humane. That doesn’t mean you failed.
