You did the right thing by getting the kitten out and seeking veterinary help. And **you are not obligated to raise it yourself**, especially if you’re already exhausted. A newborn kitten usually needs feeding and toileting help about every 2–3 hours, day and night, and an experienced neonatal foster may give it the best chance. **Right now:** - Call local cat rescues, humane societies, shelters, and TNR groups and ask specifically for an **urgent neonatal/bottle-baby foster**. Post in local rescue groups too. - Ask either vet whether they can contact a neonatal foster, provide stabilization, or recommend an emergency rescue. - Keep the kitten **gently warm** in a small box with towels and a wrapped warm-water bottle or heating pad on low under only half the box, so it can move away. - **Do not feed a cold kitten.** Once warm, use only kitten milk replacer—not cow’s milk—and never force liquid into its mouth because it can enter the lungs. - If it is limp, cold, struggling to breathe, bleeding, or unable to swallow, return to an emergency vet immediately. If no qualified foster can be found and the vets believe it is suffering with almost no realistic chance of recovery, discussing humane euthanasia is also a compassionate option—not a failure. So: **keep it only if you genuinely have the capacity and veterinary guidance. Otherwise, surrendering it promptly to an experienced foster or rescue is likely the kindest choice.** Even if it doesn’t survive, what you’ve already done means it wasn’t left alone in a dumpster.
