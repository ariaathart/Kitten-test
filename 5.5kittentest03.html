I’m really sorry — and you already did a kind, responsible thing by waiting for the mother and taking it to vets. **You don’t have to “keep” the kitten out of guilt.** A few-days-old orphan needs **round-the-clock neonatal care**, and if you’re already exhausted, the best move is likely to **get it to an experienced neonatal foster/rescue ASAP** rather than trying to do it alone. ## What I’d do now **Call/message urgently:** - Local kitten/neonatal rescues - TNR/feral cat rescue groups - Animal shelters and ask specifically for a **neonatal foster**, not just intake - Emergency vet clinics — ask if any techs/fosters bottle-feed neonates - Local Facebook/WhatsApp rescue groups: “urgent bottle baby kitten found” Use wording like: > “I found a newborn kitten alone in a dumpster. Eyes closed, only a few days old. Two vets said survival chance is low. I need an experienced neonatal foster tonight/today.” ## Until someone can take it If you have it with you: - **Keep it warm first.** Newborns can’t regulate temperature. Use a heating pad on low under half the box, or warm water bottle wrapped in a towel. It must be able to crawl away from heat. - **Do not feed if cold.** Warm it first. - Feed only **kitten milk replacer/KMR**, not cow’s milk. - Feed belly-down, slowly, never on its back. - At a few days old, it may need feeding about **every 2–3 hours**, including overnight. - After feeding, gently stimulate pee/poop with a warm damp cotton pad/tissue. If it’s **cold, limp, gasping, crying nonstop, not sucking, or milk comes from the nose**, it needs emergency help immediately. ## Should you keep it? **If “keep” means care for it forever or even solo for the next few weeks: probably no — not if you’re exhausted.** **If “keep” means keep it safe and warm temporarily while you find a neonatal foster: yes, if you can.** Handing it to someone more equipped is not abandoning it. It may genuinely be the kitten’s best chance. You’ve already given it more of a chance than it had in that dumpster.
