If it’s truly only a few days old, it may be closer to 2 hours. If milk bubbles from the nose, coughing, or struggling happens: **stop feeding** and contact an emergency vet—aspiration pneumonia is a major risk. ### C) It must pee/poop with stimulation After each feeding: - Use a **warm damp cotton pad/tissue** and gently rub the genital/anal area until it urinates (and hopefully poops). - No urine for a full cycle, straining, or a swollen belly = vet/rescue help ASAP. ### D) Track weight (this is the single best “is it winning?” indicator) If you can get a kitchen scale: - Weigh at the same time daily. - Healthy neonates typically **gain weight every day**. No gain or loss is a bad sign and needs expert input quickly. --- ## When it’s an emergency *right now* Go to an ER vet if you see: - Trouble breathing, gasping, constant crying then suddenly quiet/weak - Cold body temperature that won’t improve with warming - Refusing to eat repeatedly, extreme lethargy - Diarrhea, vomiting, bloated hard belly - Milk from nose / coughing during feeds --- ## So… should you keep it?
