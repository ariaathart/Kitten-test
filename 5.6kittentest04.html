You’ve already done something very kind by getting the kitten out and taking it to two vets. **You do not have to keep it yourself.** A kitten only a few days old usually needs feeding and toileting help every 2–3 hours, including overnight. If you’re already exhausted, the best option is to **transfer it urgently to an experienced neonatal-kitten foster or rescue**. Call the vets back and ask specifically for contacts, then try local shelters/rescues and community groups using the phrase **“urgent neonatal kitten foster needed.”** Until someone can take over: - Keep it **warm** in a small box, with a wrapped heating pad on low under only half the box. - **Never feed a cold kitten**; warm it gradually first. - Use only commercial **kitten milk replacer**, never cow’s milk. - Feed belly-down, never on its back, and don’t force liquid into its mouth—weak kittens can aspirate. Ask a vet to demonstrate and give exact amounts. - It will need gentle stimulation with a warm, damp cloth to urinate/defecate after feedings. - Keep it separate from other animals. If it is cold, limp, struggling to breathe, refusing to suckle, or has pale/blue gums, return to an emergency vet immediately. If no rescue can accept it and the vets believe it is suffering with little realistic chance of recovery, discussing **humane euthanasia is compassionate, not a failure**. Given how depleted you already feel, I would focus on finding an experienced foster rather than committing to raising it alone.
