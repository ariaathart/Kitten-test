You did a kind and responsible thing by getting the kitten to two vets. **You do not have to keep it**, especially if you’re already exhausted. A kitten only a few days old needs feeding and toileting help roughly every 2–3 hours, day and night, plus careful temperature control. That is extremely demanding even for experienced fosters. The best option is to **urgently transfer it to an experienced neonatal kitten foster or rescue**. Call: - Local cat rescues and humane societies/SPCA - Municipal shelters—ask specifically whether they have a neonatal foster program - Both vets again—ask whether a technician, rescue partner, or foster can take it - Local rescue groups online Say: **“I found a kitten only a few days old. Two vets gave it under a 15% chance of survival. I need an immediate medical/neonatal foster or surrender.”** Contact several places, because availability varies. Until someone can take it: - Keep it gently warm in a box, with a wrapped heating pad on low under **half** the box so it can move away. - **Do not feed a cold kitten.** - Use kitten milk replacer only—never cow’s milk—and feed belly-down, not on its back. - It will need gentle stimulation with a warm damp cloth after feeding to urinate and defecate. - If it is cold, limp, struggling to breathe, unable to swallow, or has pale/blue gums, return to an emergency vet immediately. Given the very poor prognosis, also ask the vet whether the kitten is suffering. If no suitable foster exists and treatment is unlikely to help, **humane euthanasia may be the kindest decision**—and choosing that would not mean you failed it. You’ve already given this kitten warmth, safety, and a chance it otherwise wouldn’t have had. Handing it to someone equipped for neonatal care is not abandonment.
