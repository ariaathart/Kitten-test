You’ve already done a lot for this kitten. **You don’t have to keep it out of guilt.** A newborn needs feeding and toileting every 2–3 hours, including overnight, and an exhausted caregiver may not be the safest option. The best next step is to **find an experienced neonatal-kitten foster urgently**. Call local kitten rescues, humane societies, shelters, and emergency vets and say: > “I have a kitten only a few days old who needs an experienced bottle-baby foster immediately. Can you take it or contact one of your fosters?” Ask the two vets whether they can contact rescue partners or accept a surrender. Confirm that any shelter has a neonatal foster program, since not all can care for newborns. ### Until someone can take over - Keep the kitten **warm**, in a small box with towels and a heating pad on low beneath only half the box. - **Never feed a cold kitten**—warm it gradually first. - Use **kitten milk replacer only**, not cow’s milk. Feed belly-down, never on its back, following the vet/formula directions. - Gently stimulate its genital area with a warm damp cotton pad after every feeding so it can urinate and defecate. - If it is limp, cold despite warming, struggling to breathe, refusing to suckle, or has pale/blue gums, return to an emergency vet immediately. Also ask the vets **why** they estimated only a 15% chance. If there is a severe illness or birth defect and the kitten is suffering, they can discuss whether continued treatment or humane euthanasia is kindest. My honest advice: **don’t commit to raising it alone if you’re already exhausted—stabilize it while urgently arranging an experienced foster or rescue.** Transferring it is responsible care, not abandoning it.
