You **don’t have to keep it**, especially if you’re already exhausted. A kitten only a few days old needs warmth, feeding every 2–3 hours around the clock, and help urinating/defecating. That is intensive care, and handing it to an experienced neonatal foster may give it the best chance. **Call the vets back now** and ask specifically for: - A neonatal kitten rescue or “bottle-baby” foster - Whether a vet tech or rescue volunteer can take it - Emergency surrender options through local shelters or humane societies - Guidance about humane euthanasia if the kitten is suffering and no capable placement exists When contacting rescues, say: **“Urgent neonatal orphan, estimated a few days old, needs an experienced bottle feeder immediately.”** Until you can transfer it: - Keep it gently warm in a box, with a wrapped heating pad on low under **half** the box. - **Never feed a cold kitten**; warm it gradually first. - Use kitten milk replacer only—no cow’s milk—and feed belly-down, never on its back or by force. - Follow the vets’ feeding and toileting instructions closely. - If it becomes limp, cold, struggles to breathe, has pale/blue gums, or cannot swallow, return to an emergency vet immediately. A 15% chance is not zero, but you are not personally required to provide round-the-clock care beyond your capacity. **Transferring it—or choosing humane euthanasia with a vet if it is suffering—is not abandoning it.** You’ve already done far more than most people by rescuing it and seeking medical help.
