I’m sorry—you’ve already done a kind and responsible thing by rescuing it and getting veterinary help. **No, you don’t have to keep and raise it yourself.** A kitten only a few days old usually needs feeding and toileting help every 2–3 hours, including overnight. If you’re already exhausted and inexperienced, the best option may be to **transfer it today to an experienced neonatal foster or rescue**. Call local shelters/rescues and specifically ask for a **“bottle-baby/neonatal kitten foster.”** The vet clinics may also know technicians or foster volunteers who take newborns. Until someone can take over: - Keep the kitten **gently warm**, with a wrapped heating pad on low under only half the box. - **Never feed a cold kitten** or force liquid into its mouth. - Use only **kitten milk replacer**, not cow’s milk, and feed belly-down—not on its back—following the vet’s directions. - Newborns need gentle stimulation with a warm damp cloth after feeding to urinate/defecate. - If it is cold, limp, struggling to breathe, refusing to suckle, or has pale/blue gums, return to an emergency vet immediately. Given the prognosis, ask the vet directly whether the kitten appears to be suffering. If no experienced caregiver is available and the vets believe recovery is extremely unlikely, **humane euthanasia may be the kindest option**—and choosing that would not mean you failed. > You do not need to destroy your own health to prove you care.
