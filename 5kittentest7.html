You’ve already helped more than most. If you keep the kitten overnight (minimum survival care) - Warmth first (critical): Kittens this young cannot regulate temperature. Keep in a small box/nest with a heating pad on low under half the space (towel layer between pad and kitten). Ears/paws should feel warm, not hot. Do not feed if the kitten feels cool—warm first for 20–30 minutes. - Feeding: Use kitten milk replacer (KMR or equivalent), never cow’s milk. Warm formula to body temp. Feed with a small bottle or syringe with the kitten tummy-down (like a sphinx), not on its back, to avoid aspiration. - Frequency: every 2–3 hours around the clock if truly newborn; tiny amounts frequently if weak. - After each feed, gently stimulate to pee/poop with a warm, damp cotton pad/tissue. - Hygiene: Keep bedding clean and dry. Wash hands and feeding tools each time. - Supplies to get today: KMR formula, small nursing bottle/syringes, heating pad or hot water bottle, digital thermometer, cotton pads, kitchen/gram scale (to track weight gain).
