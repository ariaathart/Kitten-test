If you’re keeping it briefly: immediate care 1) Warmth first (do not feed a cold kitten) - Set up a small box/carrier “nest.” Heating pad on LOW under half the box or a warm rice sock/hot water bottle wrapped in a towel. It should feel toasty, not hot. - Aim for steady warmth and quiet. If the kitten feels cool or is lethargic, warm for 20–30 minutes before offering anything by mouth. 2) Hydration/energy stopgap - Once warm and responsive, you can offer a tiny amount of warmed, unflavored Pedialyte (or water with a pinch of sugar) by dropper at the lips—just a drop at a time, no force. - Then transition to kitten milk replacer (KMR). Avoid cow’s milk. 3) Feeding basics - Use kitten bottle or syringe with a small nipple; kitten on belly (never on back). Let it suckle—don’t force. - Frequency: every 2–3 hours, including overnight. Small amounts often. If there are fleas, pick off by hand/flea comb. No topical flea meds. A quick warm, soapy wipe/bath only if the kitten is warm and stable—dry thoroughly and keep warm. 5) Monitor - Continuous crying, refusal to eat when warm, pale/white gums, gasping, bloating, diarrhea, or becoming cold/lethargic = urgent vet/rescue help. - Weighing daily (kitchen scale) helps, but don’t stress if you can’t yet—focus on warmth and gentle feeds.
