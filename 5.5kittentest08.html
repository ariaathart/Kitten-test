I’m really sorry — that’s a lot to be thrown into, especially with a kitten that young. **You should only keep it if you can realistically do round-the-clock neonatal care or can get help.** A kitten that’s only a few days old usually needs feeding every **2–3 hours, including overnight**, warmth, and help peeing/pooping. If you’re already exhausted and alone, it’s completely okay — and often kinder — to hand it over to someone equipped for this. ### Best next step Call/text **local cat rescues, shelters, TNR groups, kitten fosters, and emergency vets** and say: > “I found a neonatal kitten, a few days old, orphaned, needs immediate bottle-feeding/foster. Two vets gave poor prognosis. Can anyone take or connect me with a neonatal foster?” Also ask the vets you visited if they know a **bottle-baby foster**. Rescues often have people who specifically handle newborns. ### If you have it right now Until you can transfer it: - **Keep it warm first.** Do not feed if it feels cold. Use a heating pad on low under half the box, or warm water bottle wrapped in a towel. - Feed only **kitten milk replacer** if available — not cow’s milk. - Feed belly-down, never on its back. - After feeding, gently rub the genital area with a warm damp cotton pad/tissue to help it pee/poop. - If it becomes cold, limp, gasping, has milk from nose, blue/pale gums, or won’t swallow — that’s emergency-vet territory. ### About the survival odds Less than 15% is heartbreaking, but it doesn’t mean you caused anything or that you’re wrong to try. Newborn strays can be incredibly fragile even with perfect care. If you can’t continue, **surrendering it to a rescue/foster/vet is not abandonment**. You already did the compassionate thing by getting it out of the dumpster and seeking care.
