I’m really sorry — that’s a brutal situation to be dropped into. And yes: **it’s okay to be exhausted, and it’s okay if you can’t keep it.** If it’s only a few days old, survival usually depends on **round-the-clock neonatal care**: warmth, kitten formula feedings every 2–3 hours, stimulation to pee/poop, and quick vet help if it crashes. That’s a lot even for experienced fosters. ## Should you keep it? **Only keep it if you can realistically provide 24/7 care or quickly learn with support.** If you’re already exhausted and alone, the kitten’s best chance may be with an **experienced bottle-baby foster/rescue**. That is not abandoning it. That’s making the strongest choice for the kitten. ## What I’d do right now Call/text urgently: - Local cat rescues - Shelters and humane societies - “Bottle baby kitten foster” groups in your area - Vet clinics and emergency vets asking if they know a neonatal foster - Local Facebook/WhatsApp/Reddit animal rescue groups Say: > “I found a newborn kitten, eyes likely closed, abandoned in a dumpster. Two vets estimated low survival. I need an experienced neonatal/bottle-baby foster urgently. I can transport.” ## While you’re finding help Keep it alive and stable: - **Keep it warm first.** Heating pad on low under half the box, or warm rice sock wrapped in towel. It must be able to crawl away from heat. - **Do not feed if it feels cold.** Warm first. - Use **kitten milk replacer/KMR only** — not cow milk. - Feed belly-down, never on its back. - Newborns usually need feeding about **every 2–3 hours**, including overnight. - After each feeding, gently rub the genital/anal area with a warm damp cotton pad to make it pee/poop. - If it becomes limp, cold, gasping, won’t suck, cries constantly, has diarrhea, or has pale gums: emergency vet/rescue immediately. ## Also: ask the vet the hard question If both vets think chances are under 15%, ask directly: > “Is this kitten suffering, and is continued care humane?” Sometimes the kindest option is intensive foster care. Sometimes, if the kitten is fading or has severe issues, humane euthanasia may be the least cruel option. A vet can help judge that. ## Bottom line You don’t have to decide “forever” right now. The immediate decision is: **Can I provide intensive care tonight and for the next days/weeks?** If the honest answer is no, then **find a neonatal foster/rescue ASAP**. You already did something important by not leaving it there and getting vet opinions. That matters.
