A newborn orphan needs **intensive care every 2–3 hours, day and night**, and even with perfect care, very young kittens can still die. Choosing to hand it to a rescue, shelter, or experienced foster is **not giving up** — it may be the kindest option for both of you. ## What matters most right now A kitten only a few days old needs: - **Warmth first** — they can’t regulate body temperature - **Bottle or syringe feeding** with **kitten milk replacer** every 2–3 hours - Help to **pee/poop after every feeding** - Close monitoring for **weakness, chilling, dehydration, fading** If two vets told you survival odds are very low, that usually means the kitten is already very fragile. ## Should you keep it? Ask yourself one question: > **Can I realistically provide round-the-clock care for the next several weeks, starting immediately, without falling apart?** If the answer is **no**, then the best move is to **transfer the kitten to someone with neonatal kitten experience as soon as possible**. I need an experienced neonatal foster or medical intake immediately.”** ## If you cannot place it immediately Do only the essentials: ### 1. Keep it warm - Put the kitten in a small box/carrier with soft towels. - Use a **heating pad on LOW** under **half** the box, or a warm water bottle wrapped in cloth. - Make sure it can crawl away from heat if too warm. - **Do not feed a cold kitten.** Warm first. ### 2. Feed only proper kitten formula - Use **kitten milk replacer** only. - **Do not give cow’s milk.** - Feed **belly-down**, never on its back. - Go slowly to avoid aspiration. ### 3. Help it eliminate - After feeding, rub the genital area gently with a **warm damp cotton ball or tissue** until it pees/poops. ### 4.
