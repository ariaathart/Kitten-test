Handing it off is not abandoning it — it may give the kitten its best chance. A few-day-old kitten needs: - Feeding **every 2–3 hours, including overnight** - Constant warmth - Help peeing/pooping after every feeding - Careful monitoring for fading, dehydration, aspiration, hypothermia That’s a lot even for experienced people. ## What to do right now Call/message urgently: - Local cat rescues - Shelters and humane societies - “Neonatal kitten foster” groups - Trap-neuter-return/TNR groups - Local Facebook/Nextdoor pet rescue groups - The vets you visited — ask if they know a bottle-feeder/foster Say clearly: **“I have a days-old orphaned kitten, vets estimate low survival, I need an experienced neonatal foster immediately.”** ## If you need to keep it temporarily tonight The biggest priorities are: 1. **Warmth first** Do not feed a cold kitten. Use a heating pad on low under half the box, wrapped in towels, so it can crawl away if too warm. 2. **Kitten milk replacer only** No cow’s milk. Use KMR or another kitten formula. 3. **Belly-down feeding** Never feed on its back. Don’t squeeze milk into its mouth — aspiration can kill them. 4. **Stimulate pee/poop** After feeding, gently rub the genital area with warm damp cotton/tissue until it pees/poops. 5. **Emergency signs** Cold body, limpness, crying nonstop, not sucking, milk from nose, gasping, blue/gray gums, or no urination = emergency vet/rescue immediately. ## Emotionally: it’s okay if you can’t do this A kitten this young with a poor prognosis may not survive even with perfect care. If it dies, that doesn’t mean you failed. If you can find an experienced foster, I’d choose that over trying to shoulder it alone while exhausted. But if no one can take it immediately, keep it warm and stable while you keep calling.
