Keeping a **few-days-old orphan kitten** alive is genuinely hard even for experienced fosters. If you’re already exhausted, it’s completely reasonable to **hand it to people who do neonatal kitten care every day**. That’s not “giving up”—it’s often the *best* chance the kitten has. ## What’s most likely to help the kitten survive **Get it to a neonatal-capable rescue/foster ASAP.** Newborns need: - **Warmth 24/7** (they can’t regulate body temp) - **Feeding every 2–3 hours** around the clock - **Help peeing/pooping** after each feeding - Close monitoring for fading kitten syndrome If you can’t do that schedule (and most people can’t), a rescue with a rotating foster team is usually the best option. ## What to do right now (today) 1. **Keep the kitten warm** (most urgent). - Heating pad on **LOW** under **half** the box/carrier (so it can crawl off), wrapped in a towel. - Or a warm water bottle wrapped in cloth, refreshed often. - Target: comfortably warm to the touch, not hot. 2. **Call for a transfer—don’t wait.** - Search/call: “**neonatal kitten rescue**,” “**kitten foster**,” “**humane society**,” “**SPCA**,” “**cat rescue**,” “**TNR group**” in your city. - Call the vets you visited and ask: **“Do you know any neonatal foster/rescue who can take a bottle baby today?”** - Many areas also have community rescue networks (often coordinated via local shelters/rescues). 3. **If a shelter says they can’t take neonates**, ask: - “Do you have a **foster network** I can transfer to?” - “Can you give me **numbers for neonatal fosters** or partner rescues?” > If you can place the kitten with an experienced neonatal foster within hours, that typically gives it the best shot—especially given what two vets told you. ## If you decide to keep it (even temporarily), the “musts” Only do this if you can commit *at least until a handoff*, because missing basics can be fatal quickly. - **Warmth before food.** If the kitten feels cool/cold, **do not feed yet**—warm first (feeding a cold kitten can be dangerous). - **Use kitten milk replacer (KMR)**, not cow’s milk. - **Bottle-feed every 2–3 hours** (including overnight).
